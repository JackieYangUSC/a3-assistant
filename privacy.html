599-a3 Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 12, 2026

599-a3 is a course-project assistant (USC CSCI 599, Fall 2026). It is used only by its developer with one dedicated, temporary test Google account that holds fictitious data. It is not offered to other users.

What Google data the app accesses

  • Gmail (gmail.readonly, gmail.compose): reads messages in the test inbox to summarize them, and creates drafts. A draft is sent only after a person explicitly approves that send, and only to addresses on an allowlist. The app never sends mail on its own.
  • Google Calendar (calendar.readonly): reads events to build daily briefings and meeting reminders. The app does not change calendar data.

How the data is used

Email and calendar content is used only to answer the account owner's requests and to produce scheduled briefings and reminders, delivered to the owner's private chat. Content needed for a response is processed by a language model: either OpenAI's API or a model running on the developer's own computer. The data is not sold, used for advertising, or shared with anyone else. Use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Storage and security

  • The OAuth refresh token is stored in Google Cloud Secret Manager and is readable only by the assistant's server account.
  • Conversation history may include summaries of emails or events; it is stored on the assistant's private server and in a private Google Cloud Storage bucket used for backups.
  • Access to the server is limited to the developer and course staff.

Retention and deletion

Data is kept only for the duration of the course assignment and its grading, after which the server, its storage bucket and the stored token are deleted. Access can be revoked at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions, which immediately stops the app from reading the account.

Contact

Questions: contact the developer through the email address shown on the app's Google consent screen.
